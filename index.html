Where People and Tortoises Thrive Together

Protecting Ghana's most endangered amphibians, reptiles, and tortoises through community-led conservation.

Guardians of Tortoises in Ghana

From the Atewa Forest to the arid savannahs, we safeguard critical habitats for species found nowhere else on Earth.

Local People, Lasting Protection

Empowering local communities to become the guardians of their own natural resources and wildlife.

The Tortoise and People Project conserves tortoises and their habitats by empowering communities through research, education, and sustainable conservation.

Ghana's tortoises are among the country's most overlooked wildlife, yet they play a vital role in maintaining healthy ecosystems. The Tortoise and People Project is dedicated to protecting tortoises, conserving their habitats, and empowering communities to safeguard nature for future generations.

We combine research, habitat restoration, and community action to protect Ghana's tortoises and build a sustainable future for people and nature.

We are a passionate team of conservationists, researchers, and community organisers working to protect Ghana's most threatened amphibians and reptiles. Our work is guided by a clear vision, a focused mission, and an unwavering purpose.

Our vision

A Ghana where amphibians and reptiles thrive in healthy ecosystems, valued and protected by the communities that share their world.

Our mission

To conserve Ghana's amphibians, reptiles, and their habitats through community-led action, scientific research, and environmental education.

Our core purpose

To ensure that no amphibian or reptile species in Ghana is lost to extinction, and that their habitats remain intact for generations to come.

Impact Highlight

Spurred Tortoise
population increased
from 150 to over 640

Through dedicated habitat restoration and community-led conservation efforts, one of West Africa's most threatened tortoise species is making a remarkable comeback in Ghana's arid scrublands.

  • Established 12 community-managed safe nesting zones along critical habitat corridors
  • Trained 85 anti-poaching rangers from local communities for round-the-clock monitoring
  • Protected over 2,400 hatchlings with a 78% survival rate to adulthood
